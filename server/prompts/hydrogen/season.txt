Body characteristics also change by season. The breeding season is the best time of year to age bucks because of pronounced neck swelling and tarsal staining. You can estimate their age at other times of the year, but many characteristics are viewed relative to what they will (or did) look like during the rut. (NDA 2012)
A buck's body changes from summer to late winter through the pre-rut, the rut, and the post-rut. (TPWD 2004, Richards and Brothers)
Even general appearances change during rut; an older buck may lose up to 25 percent of his body weight due to increased activity and decreased food consumption. (MSU P2206)
Time of year should also be considered. A buck's body will change through the seasons, and can lose 25-30% of their body weight during the rut. Posturing (ears laid back, head down, stiff walk, hair erect) will make him look bigger and older. (MDWFP)
A 6 1/2-year-old will have a sagging stomach throughout most of the year, except during the post-rut due to weight loss. (TPWD 2004)
Characteristics are relative to others in your area or region. (NDA 2012)
