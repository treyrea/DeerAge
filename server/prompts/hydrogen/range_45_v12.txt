Look at the body only; ignore antlers, coat and tarsal glands. Judge from the recorded body observations. This pair may be a two-class RANGE.

MATURE-BODY RULE (this pair only): If the recorded observations describe a deep and heavy chest AND describe the belly as at, below, or nearly as deep as the chest line (only a slight rear-flank taper), then you must answer closer: OLDER. That means a single 4.5. In that case, a slight tuck, a visible neck-shoulder junction, or legs that look proportionate do not force RANGE, and you must not answer RANGE or YOUNGER. CHEST CHECK FOR THIS RULE: the chest counts as deep and heavy only if the recorded observations themselves use a word like deep, full, heavy, massive, prominent, broad or barrel for the chest or brisket. A chest and belly described only as 'similar in depth', with a 'medium', 'balanced' or 'compact' build, does NOT meet the chest check. If the chest check fails, this rule does not apply. Then: if the belly is at or nearly at the chest line AND the recorded observations note NO tuck at all (a gentle taper toward the rear flank is not a tuck), answer closer: RANGE (3.5-4.5). If any upward tuck is noted, even slight, use the YOUNGER rule below instead.

YOUNGER RULE (this pair only): If those mature-body conditions are not present, and the recorded observations show clearly young signs (a clear or moderate tuck, long legs, a thin or distinct neck, or a lean or light build) AND do not show a STRONG mature-leaning sign, then you must answer closer: YOUNGER. That means continue down. Do not answer RANGE or OLDER.

STRONG MATURE-LEANING SIGN (this pair only): a chest described as deep and heavy or massive, clearly front-heavy or heavy shoulders, or a belly at or nearly at the chest line with NO tuck noted at the flank. A belly that keeps most of the chest's depth but has a moderate or clear upward tuck at the flank or rear flank is NOT a strong mature-leaning sign; that tuck is a young sign. The neck is NEVER a strong mature-leaning sign for this pair: a thick, swollen, muscular or smoothly blending neck (with or without a visible junction) does not count and must not cause RANGE. A sign qualified as moderate, moderately, slight, slightly, somewhat or a bit (for example 'moderately thick neck', 'shoulders slightly heavier than the hindquarters') is NOT a strong mature-leaning sign and must not cause RANGE. A chest described only as deeper than the waist is a tuck, which is a young sign, not a mature-leaning sign. ONE EXCEPTION (the rut combination): if the recorded observations describe ALL THREE of these: (1) a neck called thick or swollen with NO hedge word (not moderately, slightly, somewhat or a bit thick); (2) shoulders or brisket that are fuller or heavier than the hindquarters (even when that is qualified as slightly or somewhat); and (3) an overall build NOT described as lean, light, slender or athletic, then that combination together counts as one STRONG mature-leaning sign, so with young signs present the answer is RANGE. If any one of the three is missing, the combination does not count, and no part counts alone.

RANGE RULE (this pair only): If those mature-body conditions are not present, answer closer: RANGE only when clearly young signs and a STRONG mature-leaning sign are BOTH present. That means 3.5-4.5. Do not answer RANGE when the young signs are present and there is no STRONG mature-leaning sign (use the YOUNGER rule instead). Do not force a single 3.5 or a single 4.5 when both kinds of signs are present.

The neck alone cannot decide this pair. Do not answer OLDER just because the neck is thick, swollen (including a rut-swollen neck), blending, or not. A swollen or thick neck cannot by itself make the call OLDER.

If you answer RANGE, also give hunter_cue: one short sentence for the hunter. Age it up toward 4.5 if the chest looks deep and heavy and the belly is at, below, or nearly as deep as the chest line; age it down toward 3.5 if the waist has a moderate or clear tuck or the legs look long, and there is no strong mature-leaning sign.

If neither the mature-body rule, the YOUNGER rule, nor the RANGE rule applies, answer closer: YOUNGER or OLDER as usual, with a reason. Still do not answer OLDER based only on the neck.
